## 1. End behavior in limit form
**1E.** $f(x) = -3x^5 + 2x^2 - 7$. Write the end behavior using limits:
$\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = \ ?$ and $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} f(x) = \ ?$

**1H.** $g(x) = (2x - 1)^3(x + 4)^2(5 - x)$

- (a) What are the degree and leading coefficient? *(Don't multiply it all out.)*
- (b) Write the end behavior using limits.
- (c) A different polynomial rises on the left and falls on the right. What do you know about its degree and leading coefficient?

## 2. Polynomial factoring
**2E.** Factor completely by grouping: $x^3 - 2x^2 - 9x + 18$

**2H.**

- (a) Factor completely: $x^4 - 5x^2 - 36$ *(Hint: treat it like a quadratic in $x^2$.)*
- (b) $x = 2$ is a zero of $p(x) = 2x^3 + 3x^2 - 11x - 6$. Use synthetic division to factor $p$ completely.

## 3. Roots of polynomials (including imaginary)
**3E.** Solve $x^2 - 4x + 13 = 0$.

**3H.** A polynomial with real coefficients has zeros $x = 1$ and $x = 2 - i$.

- (a) What other zero must it have, and why?
- (b) Write the polynomial of least degree with these zeros and leading coefficient 1, in standard form.
- (c) How many $x$-intercepts does its graph have?
- (d) List **all** zeros of $x^4 - 5x^2 - 36$ from 2H(a).

## 4. Multiplicity
**4E.** $f(x) = (x + 2)^2(x - 1)^3(x - 4)$

**4H.** Write the polynomial of least degree whose graph **bounces** at $x = -1$, **crosses** at $x = 2$, and has $y$-intercept $(0, -8)$.

## 7. Rational functions: end behavior
**7E.** Find each limit.

- (a) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{3x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 5}$ (b) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} \frac{4x}{x^3 + 1}$ (c) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} \frac{x^3}{2x^2 + 7}$

**7H.** $r(x) = \dfrac{x^2 + x - 4}{x - 2}$

- (a) Divide to find the slant asymptote.
- (b) Write the end behavior in limit form.

## Answer key

**1E.** Degree 5 (odd), leading coefficient $-3$ (negative): $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = -\infty$, $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to-\infty} f(x) = \infty$.

**1H.** (a) Multiply the leading terms only: $(2x)^3(x)^2(-x) = -8x^6$. Degree 6, leading coefficient $-8$. (b) Even degree, negative: $\lim_{x\to\infty} g(x) = -\infty$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty} g(x) = -\infty$. (c) The ends go opposite ways, so the degree is **odd**; it falls on the right, so the leading coefficient is **negative**.

**2E.** $x^2(x - 2) - 9(x - 2) = (x - 2)(x^2 - 9) = (x - 2)(x - 3)(x + 3)$.

**2H.** (a) Let $u = x^2$: $u^2 - 5u - 36 = (u - 9)(u + 4)$, so $(x^2 - 9)(x^2 + 4) = (x - 3)(x + 3)(x^2 + 4)$. $x^2 + 4$ doesn't factor over the reals. (b) Synthetic division by 2 on $2, 3, -11, -6$ gives $2, 7, 3$ with remainder 0. So $p(x) = (x - 2)(2x^2 + 7x + 3) = (x - 2)(2x + 1)(x + 3)$.

**3E.** $x = \dfrac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 52}}{2} = \dfrac{4 \pm 6i}{2} = 2 \pm 3i$.

**3H.** (a) $2 + i$. Complex zeros of real polynomials come in conjugate pairs. (b) $(x - 1)(x - (2 - i))(x - (2 + i)) = (x - 1)(x^2 - 4x + 5) = x^3 - 5x^2 + 9x - 5$. (c) **One** ($x = 1$); the imaginary zeros don't touch the $x$-axis. (d) From $(x - 3)(x + 3)(x^2 + 4)$: $x = 3, -3, 2i, -2i$.

**4E.** (a) $x = -2$, multiplicity 2: bounce. $x = 1$, multiplicity 3: crosses, flattening out (S-shape). $x = 4$, multiplicity 1: crosses. (b) Degree $2 + 3 + 1 = 6$, leading coefficient $+1$: $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty} f(x) = \infty$.

**4H.** (a) Bounce means even multiplicity, so $f(x) = a(x + 1)^2(x - 2)$. Then $f(0) = a(1)(-2) = -8$, so $a = 4$: $f(x) = 4(x + 1)^2(x - 2)$. (b) Degree 3, positive: $\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = \infty$, $\lim_{x\to-\infty} f(x) = -\infty$. (c) Comes up from the bottom left, touches the axis at $x = -1$ and turns back down, passes through $(0, -8)$, crosses up through $x = 2$.

**5E.** VA $x = -2$. Domain $x \ne -2$. HA $y = 1$ (same degree: $1/1$). Range $y \ne 1$. $x$-int $(3, 0)$. $y$-int $(0, -\tfrac32)$.

**5H.** $r(x) = \dfrac{2(x - 2)(x + 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)} = \dfrac{2(x - 2)}{x - 3}$ for $x \ne -2$.
Hole at $x = -2$: plug into the simplified form, $\dfrac{2(-4)}{-5} = \dfrac85$, so the hole is $(-2, \tfrac85)$. VA $x = 3$. Domain $x \ne -2, 3$. HA $y = 2$ (same degree: $2/1$). Range $y \ne 2$ and $y \ne \tfrac85$ (the hole). $x$-int $(2, 0)$; $x = -2$ is not one because it's the hole. $y$-int $(0, \tfrac43)$.

**6E.** VA $x = 1$. HA $y = 2$. $x$-int $(\tfrac12, 0)$. $y$-int $(0, 1)$. Two branches: lower-left (through the intercepts, heading down to $-\infty$ as $x\to1^-$) and upper-right (coming down from $\infty$ as $x\to1^+$, leveling to $y = 2$).

**6H.** (a) VA $x = -2$ and $x = 2$. HA $y = 0$ (bottom degree is bigger). $x$-int $(-1, 0)$. $y$-int $(0, -\tfrac14)$.
(b) Test points: $(-\infty, -2)$ negative; $(-2, -1)$ positive; $(-1, 2)$ negative; $(2, \infty)$ positive.
(c) $\lim_{x\to-2^-} r = -\infty$, $\lim_{x\to-2^+} r = \infty$, $\lim_{x\to2^-} r = -\infty$, $\lim_{x\to2^+} r = \infty$.
(d) Yes: it crosses $y = 0$ at $x = -1$. A horizontal asymptote only describes the ends, so the graph can cross it in the middle.

**7E.** (a) Same degree: ratio of leading coefficients, $3$. (b) Bottom degree bigger: $0$. (c) Top degree bigger by one: $\infty$ (it follows a slant asymptote upward).

**7H.** (a) $x^2 + x - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 3) + 2$, so $r(x) = x + 3 + \dfrac{2}{x - 2}$. Slant asymptote $y = x + 3$. (b) $\lim_{x\to\infty} r(x) = \infty$ and $\lim_{x\to-\infty} r(x) = -\infty$. (c) The numerator at $x = 2$ is $2$ (positive). From the right the denominator is a small positive number, so $\to \infty$. From the left it is a small negative number, so $\to -\infty$.
