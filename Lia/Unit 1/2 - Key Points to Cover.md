## Algebra she needs to be solid on

### Factoring (zeros, holes, asymptotes all depend on it)
- [ ] GCF first: $2x^3 - 8x = 2x(x^2 - 4) = 2x(x - 2)(x + 2)$
- [ ] Trinomials: $x^2 - x - 6 = (x - 3)(x + 2)$
- [ ] Difference of squares: $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$
- [ ] Grouping: $x^3 - x^2 + 4x - 4 = x^2(x - 1) + 4(x - 1) = (x - 1)(x^2 + 4)$
- [ ] Quadratic formula when it won't factor, including **complex answers**: $\sqrt{-4} = 2i$

### Polynomial long division
- [ ] Line up powers and fill in missing ones with $0x^k$.
- [ ] Quotient + remainder over divisor: $\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x - 1} = x + 1 + \dfrac{2}{x - 1}$

### Function notation
- [ ] $f(x + 2)$ means "plug in $x + 2$," not $f(x) + 2$.

### Solving inequalities
- [ ] Sign charts: find where the expression is 0 or undefined, then test a point in each interval.
- [ ] Flip the inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative.

### Calculator skills
- [ ] Parentheses around fractions and exponents: `(x+1)/(x-2)`, `(-2)^2` vs `-2^2`
- [ ] Regression (linear, quadratic, cubic) if her class uses it

## Intuition to build

### Rate of change (the theme of the whole course)
- [ ] **Average rate of change is just slope** between two points on a graph. Same formula as in algebra.
- [ ] **Units make it real:** "the population grew by an average of 120 people per year from 2010 to 2020."
- [ ] **Concavity is the rate of change of the rate of change.** Concave up: it's getting steeper (going up faster, or going down slower). Concave down: it's leveling off.
- [ ] Increasing and concave down is like a car still speeding up, but easing off the gas.

### Polynomials
- [ ] **Far away, only the leading term matters.** $-2x^3 + 100x^2$ looks like $-2x^3$ for big $|x|$.
- [ ] **Factors are zeros.** $(x - 3)$ in the factored form means the graph hits the axis at 3.
- [ ] **Even multiplicity bounces, odd crosses.** $(x - 1)^2$ never goes negative, so the graph can't cross there.
- [ ] Complex zeros are zeros the graph "misses": $x^2 + 1$ never touches the x-axis, but it still has two zeros, $\pm i$.

### Rational functions
- [ ] **Zero on the bottom, nonzero on top:** dividing by something tiny blows up, so there's a vertical asymptote.
- [ ] **Zero on both top and bottom (a canceling factor):** a hole, a single missing point.
- [ ] Horizontal asymptotes come from the same "leading terms win" idea as polynomial end behavior.

## Common mistakes to watch for
- [ ] Forgetting that $x - h$ means a shift **right** by $h$
- [ ] Calling a canceled factor a vertical asymptote (it's a hole)
- [ ] Forgetting conjugate pairs: if $2 + i$ is a zero, so is $2 - i$
- [ ] Multiplying out the wrong leading term for end behavior (reorder $4x^6 - x^7$ first)
- [ ] $(x + 2)^2 = x^2 + 4$ (missing the middle term $4x$)
