if you reverse a reaction, the K for the new reaction will be equal to 1/K of the old reaction 
$$
\begin{align}
 & CH_{4}(g)+2O_{2}\rightleftharpoons CO_{2} +2H_{2}O \\
 & K_{r}=\frac{1}{K_{f}} \\
 & K_{f} = \frac{[CO_{2}][H_{2}O]^{2}}{[CH_{4}][O_{2}]^2} \\
 & K_{r} = \frac{[CH_{4}][H_{2}O]^{2}}{[CO_{2}][O_{2}]^{2}}
\end{align}
$$
if you multiply a reaction by a certain number, raise the old K to the power you've multiplied the reaction by 
$$
\begin{align}
 & K_{c} = c \\
 & (1.5O_{2} \leftrightharpoons O_{3})\times 2 \\
 & \implies 3O_{2} \leftrightharpoons 2O_{3} \\
 & \implies K_{c}=c^{2}
\end{align}
$$
if you add two reactions together, multiply the two Ks 
$$
\begin{align}
 & K_{1}=c_{1} \\
 & K_{2}=c_{2} \\
 & C+H_{2}O \leftrightharpoons CO+H_{2} \\

 & CO_{2}+H_{2} \rightleftharpoons H_{2}O+CO \\
 & K_{1+2} = K_{1}K_{2}
\end{align}

Le Chatelier's Principle:
a change in any factors that determine equilibrium will cause the system to adapt in a manner to reduce the effect of that change
1. adding and removing a product
2. changing the temperature
3. changig V or P of the rxn vessel
$$
\begin{align}
 & K_{c} = \frac{\text{products}}{\text{reactants}} \\
 & R+\text{heat}\rightleftharpoons P \\
 & R\rightleftharpoons P+\text{heat} \\
 & \text{add heat to exo rxn? pushes it back.} \\
 & \text{opposite for endo rxn.} \\
 & \text{V or P ex.} \\
 & 2H_{2}+O_{2}\rightleftharpoons 2H_{2}O \\
 & \text{closed system, decrease V:} \\
 & 2H_{2}+O_{2}\implies 2H_{2}O \\
 & \text{forward rxn favored.}
\end{align}
