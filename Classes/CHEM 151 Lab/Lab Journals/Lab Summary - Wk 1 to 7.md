# CHEM 151 Lab: summary of labs through Wk 7
Back to [[Lab Journals]] · [[CHEM 151 Lab]]

Six labs have notebooks so far. The notebook numbers them Wk 1 to 4, then Making Solutions (9/28) and Equilibrium Constant (10/5), which are Wk 6 and 7 on the course schedule. Scores are from Canvas.

## 1. Calorimetry (24/25) · [[Wk 1 - Calorimetry]]
- **Goal:** use q = C_s · m · ΔT to identify two metals and measure the energy in food.
- **Part A:** boil ~50 g of each unknown metal (94.5 °C in Durango), drop it into 100 g of water in a calorimeter, and record the final temperature. Then use q_metal = −q_water to solve for C_s.
- **Part B:** burn a Frito and a Cheeto under a soda can holding 50 g of water, and use the temperature rise to get Cal/g.
- **Results:** C_s = 0.121 J/g°C for metal 3 (lead is 0.129, a good match) and 0.18 for metal 5 (nearest listed is tin at 0.222, a rough match). Fritos 1.0 Cal/g and Cheetos 0.87 Cal/g against 5.71 on the bag, so about 82 to 85 % low.
- **Why the error:** heat escapes to the air, the can isn't insulated, and the food doesn't burn completely.

## 2. Intermolecular Forces (22/25) · [[Wk 2 - Intermolecular Forces]]
- **Goal:** rank hydrogen bonding and dispersion forces.
- **Process:** wet a temperature probe with a liquid and record how far the temperature drops as it evaporates. A bigger drop means faster evaporation, so weaker intermolecular forces.
- **Drops (°C):** methanol 15.1, ethanol 11.2, propanol 4.7, butanol 1.9, pentane 14.2, hexane 10.3.
- **Fit:** for the alcohols, ΔT = −0.387M + 25.67 (M is molar mass). Heavier alcohols have stronger dispersion forces and cool less.
- **Conclusion:** hydrocarbons cooled far more than alcohols of similar mass because they have no hydrogen bonding.

## 3. Colligative Properties (24/25) · [[Wk 3 - Colligative Properties]]
- **Goal:** find the van't Hoff factor i from freezing-point depression, ΔT = K_fp · m · i with K_fp = 1.86 °C·kg/mol.
- **Process:** record cooling curves in a −6 to −13 °C ice-salt bath for pure water, 17 g sucrose in 50 g water, and 2.5 g NaCl in 50 g water. The freezing point is the peak after supercooling.
- **Results:** water froze at 0.4 °C, sucrose at −1.8 °C, NaCl at −2.7 °C. That gives i ≈ 1.19 for sucrose (ideal 1) and 1.93 for NaCl (ideal 2). The notebook has these as negative because ΔT was taken as T_solution − T_water.

## 4. Le Chatelier's Principle (23/25) · [[Wk 4 - Le Chatelier's Principle]]
- **Goal:** see that a system at equilibrium shifts to oppose a disturbance.
- **A, chromate/dichromate:** acid turns the solution yellow to orange, and NaOH reverses it.
- **B, acetic acid with methyl orange:** the indicator is pink, and adding acetate turns it yellow.
- **C, CoCl₄²⁻ ⇌ Co(H₂O)₆²⁺:** concentrated HCl turns it blue, and water returns it to pink.
- **D, NH₄Cl dissolving:** HCl precipitates the solid and warms the solution, heating dissolves it, and adding water to the solid makes the tube colder. So dissolving is endothermic.

## 5. Making Solutions and Dilutions (21/25) · [[Making Solutions and Dilutions]]
- **Goal:** prepare the stock and working solutions the next lab needs, using mass = M × V × molar mass and M₁V₁ = M₂V₂.
- **Stocks (100 mL each):** 4.04 g Fe(NO₃)₃·9H₂O for 0.1 M Fe³⁺, 0.808 g for 20 mM Fe³⁺, and 0.194 g KSCN for 20 mM SCN⁻.
- **Dilutions:** 20, 15 and 10 mL of 20 mM Fe³⁺ stock make 50 mL of 8, 6 and 4 mM. 1 mL of 20 mM SCN⁻ stock makes 100 mL of 0.2 mM.
- **Note:** the solutions must stay colorless, because color means cross-contamination.

## 6. Equilibrium Constant (19/25) · [[Equilibrium Constant]]
- **Goal:** find K_c for Fe³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ FeSCN²⁺.
- **Process:** make a Beer's law calibration from four solutions (blank plus three with limiting SCN⁻, so [FeSCN²⁺] = [SCN⁻]) at 450 nm. Then measure three test mixtures and use ICE tables.
- **Results:** calibration absorbances 0.168, 0.324 and 0.683. K_c ≈ 185, 115 and 99. An inverted K_c formula was caught and fixed in the notebook.
- **Where it drifts:** the three values should agree. K_c falling as Fe³⁺ rises is a known systematic error of the method, but pipetting precision matters here.
