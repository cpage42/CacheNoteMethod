## Part B – acetic acid with methyl orange
Reaction written as: NH₄Cl(s) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) [crossed out and overwritten]. Methyl orange acid ⇌ base structures drawn with H⁺ / OH⁻ over the equilibrium arrows (pink acid form ⇌ yellow base form).

- step 1: color is bright pink
- step 2: color turned distinctly yellow

Observations:
1. color turned pink after adding a drop of methyl orange.
2. but after adding NaCH₃COO, color turned yellow.
3. Based on LCP, forward reaction was favored; when more acetate ions were added, the solution compensated by increasing the conc. of H⁺ ions from the acetic acid, therefore increasing the pH, and decreasing the conc. of acetic acid.

## Part A – chromate / dichromate
CrO₄²⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + H₂O(l)

- step 1: K₂CrO₄ added, neon yellow color.
- step 2: 3.0 M H₂SO₄ added dropwise, turned to orange color.
- step 3: 1.0 M NaOH added dropwise, back to yellow-green color.

Balanced eq: 2 CrO₄²⁻(aq) + 2 H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + H₂O(l)

*(cont next page)*

Initial color of the solution was yellow, which indicates chromate being the dominant ion in solution. After adding sulfuric acid, the solution became orange, and was reversed back to yellow after adding NaOH. When H₂SO₄ was added, the dichromate side of the reaction was favored, indicated by the orange color of the solution, whereas when NaOH was added, it switched back to the chromate side being favored, as evidenced by the change to a yellow color. [crossed-out line: "Based on LCP then, when NaOH was added"]

So when H₂SO₄ was added, it decreased the presence of chromate, increasing dichromate as H⁺ content rose. When NaOH was added the reaction H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O occurred, decreasing chromate presence, increasing dichromate and water content. [as written]

## Part C – cobalt chloride
CoCl₄²⁻(aq) + 6 H₂O ⇌ Co(H₂O)₆²⁺(aq) + 4 Cl⁻

- step 1: 0.1 M CoCl₂ added with H₂O. color is pink, right side of rxn favored.
- step 2: conc. HCl added, color turned blue.
- step 3: distilled H₂O added. [crossed out] solution swung back to pink color.

Original color of CoCl₄²⁻ soln. was pink. After adding conc. HCl, the soln. turned blue, as the increase in Cl⁻ ions also increased the presence of Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ [as written]. Then, when H₂O was added, that in turn increased the presence of CoCl₄²⁻ ions, swinging the soln back to a pink color. The reverse reaction was favored because when Cl⁻ ions were added, the reaction compensated by decreasing CoCl₄²⁻ ion presence and increasing Co(H₂O)₆²⁺ ion presence. [as written]

## Part D – ammonium chloride
NH₄Cl(s) + H₂O(l) ⇌ NH₄⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

- step 1: conc HCl added to soln, white solid precipitated out, soln slightly warmer.
- step 2: soln heated, precipitant dissolved back into soln.
- step 3: NH₄Cl(s) added to test tube. H₂O added, soln became colder.

When HCl added, we observed the soln. heat up and precipitate NH₄Cl(s). When heat was added via a boiling water bath, the NH₄Cl(s) dissolved back into NH₄⁺ and Cl⁻. When HCl was added, the reverse reaction was favored with the added HCl raising the H₂O content [as written], in turn increasing NH₄Cl content. Based on our observations, the dissolution rxn is endothermic while the precipitation rxn is exothermic.
