## Conclusion
1. Sucrose and NaCl both drop the freezing point of water to varying degrees. By using a cold bath and graphing the temperature as each solution froze, and using the formula ΔT_fp = K_f·i·m, we were able to calculate the i values for each substance, where the i value for sucrose was i = −1.190, and i = −1.938 for NaCl. This is expected, as NaCl, specifically, when dissolved in water, creates ion-dipole bonds, which must be broken to form the crystalline lattice of frozen water, and this takes much more energy from the surroundings to happen, making the freezing process much more endothermic than pure water. Sucrose doesn't have any ion-dipole bonding action, but it does display dipole-dipole and hydrogen bonding, which still makes the freezing of water more endothermic, but to less of a degree than with the ion-dipole bonding, so our data seems accurate.
