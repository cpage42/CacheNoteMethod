## Conclusion
From our data, we found a linear relationship between the ΔT values of 4 different alcohols based on their molar masses. Starting at methanol, and adding one carbinol carbon every run, up to 1-butanol, adding a molar mass of 14.03 g/mol every time, then running a linear regression on our data, we found a relationship of (approximately) [cont →]

*(page 4)* ΔT = −0.387M + 25.671 where ΔT is the change in temperature of the sensor rod and M is the molar mass of the alcohol applied to the sensor rod.

However, when a hydrocarbon, missing the hydroxyl group on the alcohols, was applied to the rod, the temperature change was far sharper and faster than the alcohols. Adding a carbinol carbon, going from n-pentane to n-hexane, slowed the rate of change of ΔT significantly, though the rate of change was still much faster than the comparable alcohol.

We concluded, from this, that intermolecular forces play a huge role in thermochemistry. The hydrogen bonding and dipole-dipole bonding of the alcohols takes far more energy to break than the pure dispersion forces of the two hydrocarbons, meaning that the hydrocarbons take much more energy from the rod than the alcohols do.
