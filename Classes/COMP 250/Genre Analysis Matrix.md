## Part 2: patterns across all eight

### Rhetorical situation
| Question | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Audience | two at once: loyal fans, and critics or press watching for sincerity |
| What readers know and want | they know the backstory, and no description explains what happened. they want a sign of sincerity and what happens next |
| Time spent | seconds (S2 under a minute if expanded, S5 none). only a few lines show before "more," so what matters goes first |
| Purpose | frame the video, manage the creator-audience relationship. some also keep the business running |

### Typical content
| Question | Pattern |
|---|---|
| What gets emphasized | feelings, action, or business. dropping the usual links is itself a signal of sincerity |
| Appropriate info | gratitude, vague admission, promises, ways to act. not appropriate: detailed accounts of the wrongdoing, heavy promotion. plug-keepers (S1, S8) risk looking like they profit. sherman in *jezebel* (24 aug 2018): these videos mainly protect fame and income |
| Evidence | sincerity is shown, not argued: emotion, action, absence of ads |

### Structure
| Question | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Length | 0 (S5) to about 1,440 characters (S3), never more than a few short paragraphs (S1 has three). S3 is long from its link list, S2 from one big paragraph plus links |
| Sequence | greeting or thanks → admission or intent → promise → sign-off → links |
| Structure and purpose | front-loading puts the sincere part in the lines shown before "more." links go last where fewer people see them |

### Style and language
| Question | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Formality | mostly informal and personal, like writing to friends. S6 is the formal one and is written as a business |
| Vocabulary | accountability and therapy words (honest, open, accountable, reflection), youtube terms (demonetized, clickbait), fan nicknames ("Sisters") |
| Visual | plain text only, so emphasis is caps, punctuation, line breaks. no emojis in any of the eight (not yet compared with their usual descriptions) |

## Takeaways for the essay
- **conventions all eight share:** description under the video on the creator's own channel, public and free. short (0 to 1,440 characters), mostly informal and first-person, never retells what happened because readers already know. the part meant to convince comes first, before "more," and links come last. nobody teaches the rules, creators pick them up from earlier apologies. S7 is a parody, but it only works because the formula is recognizable, so it's evidence for the conventions, not a normal sample.
- **where they differ, and why:** how they signal sincerity. emotion: S2, S8. action: S3. process: S4. absence: S5. corporate remedy: S6, because the problem was a product, not behavior. commercial stance too: S1, S7, S8 promote something, S3 says it's demonetized, S5 strips everything, S2 keeps channel and social links but sells nothing, S4 has no links. timing may matter (S2 in 2017 leans on emotion, S3 in 2020 leans on action), but check before arguing it.
- **working thesis:** youtube apology video descriptions are short, front-loaded texts that signal sincerity to fans and critics at once. because readers already know the story and read only the first lines, creators show sincerity instead of arguing for it, through emotion, action, or deliberate absence. the unwritten rule that a plug undercuts sincerity shows up mainly when creators break it (S1, S8).
