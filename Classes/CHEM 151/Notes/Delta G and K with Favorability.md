at equilibrium:
$$
\begin{align}
 & \Delta G\degree=-RT\ln\frac{[\text{products}]}{[\text{reactants}]} \\ \\
 & \Delta G\degree=-RT\ln K_{eq} \\ \\
 & \forall\Delta G \text{ is nonstandard, use Q (NOT at eqbrm)} \\ \\
 & \Delta G=\Delta G\degree+RT\ln Q \\ \\
 & \Delta G=-RT\ln K_{eq}+RT\ln Q \\ \\
 & \Delta G=RT\ln \frac{Q}{K}
\end{align}
$$
So how do we get unfavorable reactions to move forward?
1. Maintain ${}Q<K{}$ (i.e. we can push the reaction forward using Le Chatlier's principle)
2. Couple unfavorable reaction to highly favorable reaction
3. Increase Temperature
