40# 11.2 Equation Sheet: Magnetic Fields and Lines
Class: [[PHYS 218]] · Chapter: [[Ch 11 - Magnetic Forces and Fields]]

This covers 11.2 plus the things HW 11.2 pulls in: circular motion, the velocity selector and the mass spectrometer.

## Force on a moving charge
| What | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetic force | $\vec F = q\,\vec v\times\vec B$ | Only on **moving** charges |
| Magnitude | $F = qvB\sin\theta$ | $\theta$ = angle between $\vec v$ and $\vec B$ |
| Max / zero | $F_{max} = qvB$ when $\vec v\perp\vec B$; $F = 0$ when $\vec v\parallel\vec B$ | |
| Lorentz force | $\vec F = q\vec E + q\,\vec v\times\vec B$ | Both fields at once |

**Right-hand rule 1:** point your fingers along $\vec v$, curl them toward $\vec B$, and your thumb gives $\vec F$ for a **+** charge. **Flip it for electrons.**

**Cross products:** $\hat x\times\hat y=\hat z$, $\hat y\times\hat z=\hat x$, $\hat z\times\hat x=\hat y$. Reversing the order flips the sign.

**Notation:** ⊙ = out of the page, ⊗ = into the page.

## Units
- $1\ \text{T} = 1\ \dfrac{\text{N}}{\text{C·m/s}} = 1\ \dfrac{\text{N}}{\text{A·m}} = 1\ \dfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{C·s}}$
- $1\ \text{G} = 10^{-4}\ \text{T}$. Earth's field is about $0.5$ G $= 5\times10^{-5}$ T.

## Field lines
- The field is tangent to the line; **outside** a magnet the lines run N → S.
- Closer lines mean a stronger field.
- Lines never cross.
- Lines are **closed loops**, because there are no magnetic monopoles.

## Circular motion (used in HW 11.2)
The magnetic force is always $\perp\vec v$, so it **does no work**: speed stays constant and only the direction changes.

| What | Equation |
|---|---|
| Force = centripetal | $qvB = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ |
| Radius | $r = \dfrac{mv}{qB}$ |
| Period (no $v$!) | $T = \dfrac{2\pi m}{qB}$ |
| Frequency | $f = \dfrac{qB}{2\pi m}$, $\ \omega = \dfrac{qB}{m}$ |
| Helix (v not ⟂ B) | $r = \dfrac{mv_\perp}{qB}$, pitch $p = v_\parallel T$ |

## Velocity selector and mass spectrometer
| What | Equation |
|---|---|
| Undeflected speed ($qE = qvB$) | $v = \dfrac{E}{B}$ → $E = vB$ |
| Speed from an accelerating voltage | $qV = \tfrac12 mv^2 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2qV/m}$ |
| Mass from the radius (2nd field $B_0$) | $m = \dfrac{qB_0 r}{v}$ |

## Constants and conversions
| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| $e$ | $1.602\times10^{-19}$ C |
| $1$ u (amu) | $1.6605\times10^{-27}$ kg |
| $m_p$ | $1.673\times10^{-27}$ kg $= 1.0073$ u |
| $m_n$ | $1.675\times10^{-27}$ kg $= 1.0087$ u |
| $m_e$ | $9.109\times10^{-31}$ kg |
| Alpha ($^4$He nucleus) | $m = 4.0015$ u $= 6.645\times10^{-27}$ kg, $\ q = +2e$ |
| $1$ eV | $1.602\times10^{-19}$ J |

**Always convert to SI** (kg, C, m/s, T) before plugging into $r = mv/qB$. Then $r$ comes out in meters.

## Common mistakes
- Using the **atom's** mass for an alpha (4.0026 u) instead of the nucleus (4.0015 u).
- Forgetting that an alpha is **2e**, not e.
- Forgetting to flip the RHR for electrons.
- Using $qvB$ when $\vec v\parallel\vec B$. The force is 0 there.
